## Overview

This repository contains a comprehensive corporate information confidentiality security check manual. The document addresses the practical and organisational dimensions of information security in office environments, with a focus on **routine self-audit** rather than technical countermeasures.

Covers personnel behaviour, meeting-room security, device and network controls, electromagnetic protection, routine checklists, and mandatory red-line prohibitions. Suitable for government and enterprise entities, classified offices, technology firms, and project-oriented companies.

## Introduction

Instances of information leakage in enterprises and government entities are seldom the product of major operational error. They do not ordinarily arise from an insider trafficking in documents, nor from a remote attack by an external actor. Rather, they are embedded in the routines of daily office work — in the minor habits that all personnel take for granted.

Such concealed vulnerabilities silently appropriate the content of meetings, project data, negotiation intelligence and core materials. By the time the leakage is identified, it has typically become impossible either to trace its origin or to contain its consequences.

The present article examines, in a single treatment, the eight categories of office confidentiality hazard that are most frequently overlooked, most high-risk and most effectively concealed. Administrative staff, confidentiality officers, technical operations personnel and management are advised to retain the document and to employ it as a basis for self-inspection.

---

## 1. Mobile Telephones: The Principal Mobile Espionage Terminal

In the great majority of cases, the foremost source of leakage is the mobile telephone.

It is commonly assumed that refraining from photography or video recording affords adequate protection. The reality is the reverse:

- Smart watches and Bluetooth earphones are capable of capturing the audio of an entire meeting at a distance.
- In standby mode, certain applications are able to record audio and determine location silently in the background.
- The introduction of a mobile telephone into a classified meeting room or negotiation room is equivalent to a live broadcast.

**Compliance baseline.** In classified and other significant negotiating contexts, mobile telephones must be stored collectively, powered down and secured in a cabinet; they must not be carried on the person.

---

## 2. Wireless Keyboards and Mice, and Wireless Printers: Concealed Electromagnetic Leakage Channels

For reasons of convenience, many offices equip themselves entirely with wireless keyboards, wireless mice and wireless printing devices.

Industry data establish that wireless devices emit electromagnetic signals continuously. Specialised interception equipment is able, from outside the building, from a lower floor or from adjacent premises, to capture such radiated signals and to reconstruct keystroke records, printed content and screen images.

Office equipment that appears entirely ordinary thus constitutes an open port of leakage.

**Recommended safeguards.** In classified posts and core office areas, equipment should be replaced uniformly with wired keyboards and mice and wired printers; Bluetooth, wireless and remote-sharing functions should be disabled, thereby closing the leakage risk at the physical level.

---

## 3. Messenger Apps, Cloud Drives and Scanning Applications: Invisible Leakage through the Cloud

It is common practice among employees to transmit documents directly through messenger apps, to retain screenshot backups, to archive materials by means of telephone scanning, and to store information in cloud drives.

This constitutes the most frequent and most damaging leakage behaviour within an enterprise. All telephone scanning software, optical character recognition mini-programs and third-party cloud drives default to cloud synchronisation and background upload. What is presumed to be a local save has in fact already been transmitted to an external server; should the platform's data be compromised or harvested by automated crawlers, the enterprise's core data is left entirely unprotected.

**Inviolable rule.** Classified material must not be placed online, and online material must not be classified. Internal sensitive materials must not be transmitted, backed up, scanned or stored by means of any internet-connected tool.

---

## 4. Unfamiliar Word and PDF Files: Concealed Macro-Virus Espionage

A risk that has recently become prevalent in government and enterprise settings is that of malicious documents disguised as notices, contracts or announcements.

Attackers fabricate official red-header formats and official sign-offs, deliver the documents by electronic mail or group chat, and induce personnel to open them. Upon activation of "Enable Content" or "Enable Macros", a program is implanted in the background and proceeds silently to appropriate all files on the computer, together with chat records and desktop materials, which are then periodically transmitted externally without detection.

**Self-inspection principle.** For unfamiliar files, macros must under no circumstances be enabled, editing must not be permitted, and content of unknown provenance must not be opened.

---

## 5. Idle Sockets, Ornaments and Decorative Objects: Concealed Pinhole Espionage Devices

Following the entry of external personnel, construction workers or visitors into office areas and meeting rooms, socket apertures, plants, ornamental objects, the vicinity of projectors and the area beside waste bins constitute preferred locations for the concealment of espionage devices.

Micro-recorders and pinhole cameras are extremely small, require no network connection and offer extended standby operation; they are exceedingly difficult to identify by visual inspection and are directed specifically at the capture of meeting and negotiation intelligence and internal decision-making information.

Where an institution conducts no environmental inspection over a prolonged period, the position is equivalent to maintaining a permanently open intelligence port.

---

## 6. Departed Accounts and Idle Permissions: Long-Term Hidden Apertures

The following latent risks are readily overlooked by enterprises:

- Accounts of departed employees that have not been cancelled
- Permissions of outsourced and temporary personnel that have not been reclaimed
- Computers, cloud drives and system accounts that have remained idle for extended periods

Such "zombie permissions" are principal points of breach for external attackers and constitute invisible channels for internal leakage.

**Management requirement.** On the day of any personnel change, accounts must be cancelled, permissions reclaimed and device data cleared; audits of the accounts and permissions of the entire staff must be conducted at regular intervals.

---

## 7. Residual Data in Printers and Photocopiers: Continuous Leakage

The great majority of office printing devices incorporate built-in hard drives, and all print, copy and scan records are retained within the device over extended periods.

Where equipment is externally repaired, scrapped or resold without professional data destruction, all previous classified documents, contracts, drawings and reports remain capable of recovery and extraction, giving rise to leakage on a mass scale.

---

## 8. Office Electromagnetic Radiation: Interception Through Walls

It is frequently assumed that the closing of windows, the drawing of curtains and the locking of doors affords adequate security. What is overlooked is leakage through electromagnetic radiation.

Computers, monitors, servers, switches and projectors all emit radiated signals during operation. Specialised equipment is capable of receiving such signals at a distance and reconstructing screen content and textual data, an effect that remains operative through walls and between floors.

Core classified rooms and decision-making meeting rooms must therefore be subjected to electromagnetic environment testing, signal shielding and compliant protective measures.

---

## Concluding Remarks

Genuine confidentiality security consists not in the avoidance of conspicuous incidents, but in the elimination of every minor habitual vulnerability.

Some ninety-nine per cent of leakage originates in everyday negligence rather than in deliberate sabotage. Self-inspection, self-correction and routine testing constitute the baseline of enterprise information security.
